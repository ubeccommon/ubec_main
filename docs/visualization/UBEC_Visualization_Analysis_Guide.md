## Overview

The UBEC Holonic Visualizer generates seven distinct analyses that provide actionable insights for bioregional economic governance. Each visualization serves a specific purpose in understanding network health, identifying opportunities, and guiding resource allocation decisions.

This guide explains what each analysis reveals and how community stewards can use these insights to strengthen the Ubuntu Bioregional Economic Commons.

---

## 1. Score Distribution Histogram

### What It Shows

A histogram displaying how composite holonic scores are distributed across all evaluated accounts in the network. The x-axis shows score ranges (0.0 to 1.0), and the y-axis shows the number of accounts in each range. Bars are color-coded by holonic category thresholds.

**Color Coding:**
- 🟣 **Exemplar** (≥0.8): Soft Amethyst - Wisdom & Leadership
- 🟢 **Integrator** (0.6-0.8): Sage Green - Growth & Balance
- 🔵 **Contributor** (0.4-0.6): Sky Blue - Clarity & Cooperation
- 🟠 **Participant** (0.2-0.4): Soft Terracotta - Community & Warmth
- ⚪ **Observer** (<0.2): Soft Slate - Neutrality & Potential

### Decision-Making Applications

| Pattern Observed | What It Means | Recommended Action |
|------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Left-skewed** (most accounts low scores) | Network is young or engagement is limited | Launch onboarding campaigns; increase UBECrc distribution incentives |
| **Right-skewed** (most accounts high scores) | Mature, engaged network | Consider raising category thresholds; expand to new bioregions |
| **Bimodal** (peaks at low and high) | Two distinct participant groups | Investigate barriers preventing middle-tier advancement |
| **Normal distribution** (bell curve) | Healthy, balanced network | Maintain current strategies; focus on outlier support |
| **Mean line far from median** | Score inequality exists | Review distribution fairness; identify systemic barriers |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Is our network growing in engagement over time?
2. Are there barriers preventing participants from advancing?
3. Is the holonic scoring system appropriately calibrated?
4. Where should we focus community development resources?

## 2. Category Distribution Pie Chart

### What It Shows

A donut-style pie chart showing the proportion of accounts in each holonic category. Each slice represents one category with its percentage of the total network.

**Categories (Ubuntu Philosophy):**
- **Exemplar**: Demonstrates highest Ubuntu alignment; potential community leaders
- **Integrator**: Actively building connections; bridge-builders in the network
- **Contributor**: Regular positive participation; reliable community members
- **Participant**: Engaged but developing; growing into fuller participation
- **Observer**: Present but minimal activity; potential for activation

### Decision-Making Applications

| Distribution Pattern | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|---------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **>50% Observers** | Low activation rate | Review onboarding process; simplify initial engagement |
| **<5% Exemplars** | Leadership gap | Mentorship programs; pathway clarity to Exemplar status |
| **Growing Integrators** | Healthy network maturation | Support with governance roles; UBECtt eligibility |
| **Shrinking Contributors** | Middle-tier exodus | Survey for pain points; improve Contributor benefits |
| **Balanced across all** | Ideal mature network | Document and replicate success patterns |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Do we have enough leaders (Exemplars) to guide community governance?
2. Is the pathway from Observer to Exemplar working?
3. Which category needs the most support right now?
4. Are we retaining engaged members or losing them?

## 3. Radar Chart (Dimension Scores)

### What It Shows

A five-axis spider/radar chart displaying scores across all holonic dimensions for the top-performing accounts. Each axis represents one dimension, and the filled area shows how balanced or specialized each account's contribution is.

**The Five Holonic Dimensions:**

| Dimension | Element | What It Measures |
|-----------|---------|------------------|
| **Autonomy Integration** | 💨 Air | Self-sufficiency balanced with community participation |
| **Multi-Scale Participation** | 💧 Water | Engagement across local, regional, and network levels |
| **Regenerative Impact** | 🌱 Earth | Contribution to ecological and economic regeneration |
| **Network Contribution** | 🔥 Fire | Value added to the collective through transactions and governance |
| **Ubuntu Alignment** | 🟣 Spirit | Embodiment of "I am because we are" philosophy |

### Decision-Making Applications

| Shape Pattern | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Large, balanced pentagon** | Well-rounded high performer | Candidate for governance roles; mentor assignment |
| **Spike in one dimension** | Specialist contributor | Match with complementary accounts; role-specific tasks |
| **Small, balanced shape** | Consistent but limited engagement | Growth pathway coaching; remove participation barriers |
| **Irregular, jagged shape** | Inconsistent engagement | Identify which dimensions need support |
| **Network average (blue line) is small** | Overall low engagement | Network-wide activation campaign needed |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Are our top performers balanced or specialized?
2. Which dimensions are strongest/weakest network-wide?
3. Who should be paired together for complementary strengths?
4. What skills or behaviors should we incentivize?

## 4. Time-Series Trend Chart (30-Day)

### What It Shows

A line chart tracking daily average composite scores over the past 30 days. Includes confidence intervals (±1 standard deviation shaded area), min-max range, and a linear trend line with slope calculation.

**Visual Elements:**
- **Solid line**: Daily average composite score
- **Shaded band**: ±1 standard deviation (where most accounts fall)
- **Light shading**: Full min-max range
- **Dashed line**: Linear trend with slope value

### Decision-Making Applications

| Trend Pattern | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Upward slope (positive)** | Network health improving | Continue current strategies; document what's working |
| **Downward slope (negative)** | Declining engagement | Urgent review of recent changes; community feedback session |
| **Flat line** | Stable but stagnant | Innovation needed; new programs or incentives |
| **High volatility (wide bands)** | Inconsistent participation | Stabilize with regular touchpoints; predictable rewards |
| **Narrowing bands over time** | Network converging | Healthy maturation; consider new challenges |
| **Sudden drop** | Event-triggered decline | Investigate specific date; crisis response if needed |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Is our network getting healthier or declining?
2. Did a specific event (distribution, announcement) impact scores?
3. How predictable is our network's behavior?
4. Are we on track to meet quarterly goals?

## 5. Dimension Correlation Matrix

### What It Shows

A heatmap showing statistical correlations between all five holonic dimensions. Values range from -1.0 (perfect negative correlation) to +1.0 (perfect positive correlation). Color coding: green = positive correlation, red = negative correlation.

**Reading the Matrix:**
- **+0.7 to +1.0**: Strong positive correlation (dimensions move together)
- **+0.3 to +0.7**: Moderate positive correlation
- **-0.3 to +0.3**: Weak or no correlation (independent dimensions)
- **-0.3 to -0.7**: Moderate negative correlation
- **-0.7 to -1.0**: Strong negative correlation (trade-off exists)

### Decision-Making Applications

| Correlation Finding | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|--------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **High correlation between all** | Dimensions may be redundant | Review scoring algorithm; ensure dimensions are distinct |
| **Ubuntu & Network strongly correlated** | Community philosophy drives participation | Lead with Ubuntu messaging; values-first onboarding |
| **Autonomy negatively correlated with others** | Self-sufficiency at cost of community | Reframe autonomy as "contribution capacity" |
| **Regenerative isolated (low correlations)** | Environmental impact separate from social | Create programs linking ecological and social action |
| **Multi-Scale & Network strongly correlated** | Breadth of engagement predicts value | Encourage cross-bioregion participation |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Are our five dimensions truly measuring different things?
2. What behaviors tend to go together?
3. Are there trade-offs we're inadvertently creating?
4. Which dimension is the best predictor of overall success?

## 6. Category Performance Comparison

### What It Shows

A grouped bar chart comparing average scores across all five dimensions for each holonic category. Each category has five bars (one per dimension), allowing direct comparison of how categories differ in their strengths.

**Reading the Chart:**
- Bar height = average score for that category in that dimension
- Compare same-color bars across categories to see dimension patterns
- Compare bars within a category to see that category's profile

### Decision-Making Applications

| Pattern Observed | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Exemplars strong in Ubuntu, weak in Network** | Leaders need connectivity support | Peer networking events for Exemplars |
| **Observers low across all dimensions** | True newcomers or inactive | Differentiate: onboard newcomers, re-engage inactive |
| **Contributors match Integrators except one dimension** | Clear advancement pathway | Focus development on the gap dimension |
| **All categories similar in Regenerative** | Dimension not differentiating | Strengthen regenerative scoring criteria |
| **Large gap between Participant and Contributor** | Advancement barrier exists | Create bridge programs; mentorship pairing |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. What distinguishes each category from others?
2. Where should each category focus to advance?
3. Are our category thresholds set appropriately?
4. What's the clearest pathway from Observer to Exemplar?

## 7. Transaction Network Visualization

### What It Shows

A network graph where nodes represent accounts and edges represent transaction relationships. Node size reflects composite score (larger = higher score), node color indicates holonic category, and edge thickness shows transaction volume between accounts.

**Visual Elements:**
- **Node size**: Proportional to composite score
- **Node color**: Holonic category (using Ubuntu palette)
- **Edge thickness**: Number of transactions between accounts
- **Edge arrows**: Direction of token flow
- **Clusters**: Groups of frequently-transacting accounts

### Decision-Making Applications

| Network Pattern | Interpretation | Strategic Response |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Central hub(s) with many connections** | Key network facilitators | Support and protect these nodes; succession planning |
| **Isolated clusters** | Siloed sub-communities | Bridge-building programs; cross-cluster incentives |
| **Many unconnected nodes** | Low transaction activity | Simplify transaction process; create transaction occasions |
| **High-score nodes isolated** | Leaders not transacting | Investigate why; may indicate hoarding concerns |
| **Dense, interconnected mesh** | Healthy circular economy | Celebrate and document; model for other bioregions |
| **One-directional flows** | Distribution without circulation | Encourage reciprocal exchange; UBECrc programs |

### Key Questions This Analysis Answers

1. Is value circulating or accumulating?
2. Who are the critical connectors in our network?
3. Are there isolated groups we should bridge?
4. Does transaction behavior match holonic scores?

## Using Multiple Analyses Together

### Comprehensive Network Health Assessment

Combine insights from multiple visualizations for deeper understanding:

| If You See... | Combined With... | It Suggests... |
|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Declining trend | Shrinking Integrator category | Bridge-builders leaving; relationship crisis |
| High Observers | Sparse network graph | Onboarding not leading to engagement |
| Strong Ubuntu-Network correlation | Dense network clusters | Values driving behavior (positive sign) |
| Right-skewed scores | Few transaction edges | High scores not translating to economic activity |
| Balanced radar charts | Growing trend line | Healthy, sustainable network growth |

### Quarterly Review Framework

1. **Start with Trend**: Is the network improving, stable, or declining?
2. **Check Distribution**: Where are accounts concentrated?
3. **Examine Categories**: Which groups need attention?
4. **Review Correlations**: Are dimensions working as intended?
5. **Map Network**: Is value flowing appropriately?
6. **Compare Performance**: What distinguishes successful accounts?
7. **Set Priorities**: Based on findings, what are top 3 actions?
